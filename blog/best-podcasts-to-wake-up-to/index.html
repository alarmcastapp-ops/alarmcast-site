Podcasts that work as an alarm

What decides it is the time the episode lands, not what the show is about. We measured publication times and episode lengths from the publishers' own feeds on 21 August 2026.

By the Alarmcast team Published 21 August 2026 About 8 minutes

Pick a daily news show as your alarm and there is a decent chance you will wake up to yesterday's episode. Not because anything failed, but because you set the alarm for 6 am and the episode you wanted was published at 6:30.

This is the part nobody writing about morning podcasts mentions. A show's publication time decides whether it can be your alarm, and it varies by hours between shows that look interchangeable on a list.

The short answer

For a 6 am alarm you want a show that publishes in the small hours of your own timezone. Measured from the publishers' own feeds: Today in Focus lands 03:00 London, The Indo Daily 01:05 Dublin, ABC News Daily 02:00 Sydney, Short Wave 03:00 New York, and BirdNote Daily 00:00 Pacific. All of those are comfortably finished before a normal alarm.

The ones to watch are the shows that land close to when you wake. NPR's Up First has a median publication time of 06:00 ET, The Daily's is 05:45 ET and the BBC's Global News Podcast morning edition is 05:46 London. Those are fine for a 7 am alarm and marginal for a 6 am one. Two more traps: The News Agents and Newscast sound like morning shows and are actually published in the late afternoon, and most comedy podcasts run 40 to 90 minutes and arrive at lunchtime.

Why the clock matters more than the show

An alarm that plays the newest episode has to find one that exists. Alarmcast picks and downloads the episode roughly 30 minutes before the alarm, so anything published inside that window arrives too late for this morning and waits for tomorrow. Any app that streams at alarm time has the same problem in a different shape: it can only play what the feed is carrying at 6 am.

There is a second wrinkle. The schedule a publisher states and the time its feed actually stamps the episode are not always the same, and an alarm reads the stamp. NPR's own feed description says Up First is "available weekdays at 6:30 a.m. ET". Across its last 55 weekday episodes, the feed's own timestamps have a median of 06:00 ET, with the earliest tenth at 05:46 and the latest tenth at 06:19. The show arrives earlier than advertised and it varies by half an hour.

So the useful question about any show is not "is it a morning show", it is "what time was yesterday's episode stamped".

If you do not want news first thing

Not everyone wants the state of the world before their feet touch the floor. Four measured options that suit an early alarm:

  • BirdNote Daily, seven days a week at 00:00 Pacific, a median of 1.8 minutes. Short enough that it ends before you are properly upright, which suits people who want a sound rather than a sitting.
  • Everything Everywhere Daily, every day at 04:00 US Central, about 15 minutes. One self-contained history or science story, and no news cycle.
  • StoryCorps, fortnightly on Tuesdays at 03:00 ET, about 17 minutes. Ordinary people interviewing each other, which is about as far from a siren as audio gets.
  • Skeptoid, weekly on Tuesdays at 02:00 Pacific, about 17 minutes.

Two more worth knowing about, with a timing catch. Circle Round publishes Tuesdays at 15:00 ET, and the Nature Podcast publishes its main episode Wednesday at 16:00 London. Both land in the afternoon, so they suit an alarm the following morning rather than the same day.

Comedy is the hardest fit, with one exception

Comedy podcasts are long and mostly weekly. Measured medians: Comedy Bang Bang 88.5 minutes, Judge John Hodgman 64.2, My Brother, My Brother And Me 57.2, Sawbones 39.2. Several publish at lunchtime, and a weekly show means six mornings of the same episode.

The exception is timing rather than length. No Such Thing As A Fish publishes at 23:55 London on Thursdays, so it is sitting finished on your phone for a Friday morning alarm, which is arguably the right morning for it. The same logic gives you MBMBaM on a Tuesday, since it lands Monday at noon ET, and Sawbones on a Wednesday.

The shows that can never be late

A finished podcast has one quiet advantage as an alarm: nothing new can arrive late, because nothing new arrives. The History of Rome ended in 2012 and leaves 193 episodes in its feed at a median of 26 minutes. Long-running archives work the same way, and Lore, Myths and Legends and The Rest Is History all carry hundreds of episodes.

Set an alarm to carry on through a back catalogue rather than to chase the newest episode, and the publication-time problem disappears entirely.

One caveat on old shows. When we checked the 385 feeds in Alarmcast's shipping catalogue on 7 August 2026, three were broken outright and 50 had simply stopped publishing. A show that has not released anything for two years is fine as an archive and useless if you were expecting something new.

How to check any show yourself

Open the show in whatever podcast app you use and look at the timestamps on the last five or six episodes, not at the description. You are looking for three things: what time of day they land, whether that time is consistent, and whether the gap between episodes matches what the show claims. Ten seconds of that tells you more than any recommendation list, this one included.

Then set the alarm an hour after the show's usual time for a week. If it is a show you actually want, the hour is worth it.

Questions people actually ask

What is the best podcast to wake up to?

For an early alarm, a daily show that publishes in the small hours of your timezone. Measured examples include The Indo Daily at 01:05 Dublin, ABC News Daily at 02:00 Sydney, Today in Focus at 03:00 London and Short Wave at 03:00 ET. If you would rather not start with news, BirdNote Daily publishes at midnight Pacific and runs under two minutes, and Everything Everywhere Daily publishes at 04:00 US Central and runs about 15 minutes.

Why does my alarm play yesterday's episode?

Because today's had not been published when the alarm needed it. An alarm has to download or fetch an episode that already exists, so a show stamped 06:30 cannot be the content of a 06:00 alarm. Check the timestamps on recent episodes and either move the alarm later or pick an earlier-publishing show.

Is Up First good as an alarm?

It suits a 7 am alarm better than a 6 am one. NPR describes it as available weekdays at 6:30 a.m. ET, and its feed timestamps have a median of 06:00 ET with roughly half an hour of variation either side. It runs about 13 minutes on weekdays.

How long should a wake-up podcast be?

Long enough to still be talking when you get up, which for most people means at least 10 minutes. Daily news briefings sit between 13 and 28 minutes, which fits. Comedy and interview shows commonly run 40 to 90 minutes, so you will only ever hear the opening.

Do these publication times change?

Yes. These were measured on 21 August 2026 from each publisher's own feed, and shows change schedules, run summer repeats and shift with daylight saving. Treat the table as a snapshot and check the show yourself before you rely on it.
